# Intent: Bounded confirmation that a prerequisite holds, before acting on it

## Problem

`input` reports that a keystroke was delivered. Nothing in ai-mirror can report
that the thing the keystroke was meant to cause has actually happened. A caller
is left with two options and both are bad.

It can sleep a guessed interval and continue. The guess is right most of the
time, and the times it is wrong are silent. Timed on p620, pressing the key
that opens the package panel: `input` returned at 164 ms, and the panel's
`openlayer` event arrived at 225 ms. (One sample, on an idle machine. It is an
illustration that the gap is real, not a bound on it — under load it will be
worse, and the ordering is not guaranteed either way.)

Or it can take a screenshot and look. That works, and costs a round trip and a
model's attention to answer what is usually a yes or no. It is expensive enough
that callers skip it, which returns them to guessing.

What is missing is not events. It is any way to ask "is the thing I depend on
actually true yet?" and get a bounded answer.

## What I got wrong on the way here

Recorded because the correction is most of the value in this document.

**First error.** In #3 I wrote that whether a panel is open "can only be
answered by taking a screenshot", and filed olafkfreund/nixarchy#749 asking
nixarchy to grow an observation API. Both were wrong. `hyprctl layers -j`
answers it directly, today:

```
panel closed → no namespace matching nixarchy-pkg-menu
panel open   → 'nixarchy-pkg-menu' present
```

**Second error.** Having found the event socket, I wrote the first draft of
this intent around waiting for `openlayer`, and claimed it would make the
`azure-cli` failure "impossible to reach silently". An independent review
(OpenAI Codex, `gpt-6-astra`) pointed out that this does not follow, and it is
right. In that incident the panel **did** open; the click that missed was on
the filter box *inside* an already-open panel. An `openlayer` check would have
passed and every harmful step would still have run.

So the honest claim is much narrower than the one I made twice.

The pattern in both errors is the same: I checked one surface, found it
lacking, and built on that instead of checking the one next to it.

## Proposed outcome

A caller can state a prerequisite and receive a bounded, honest answer before
sending actions that depend on it:

- the package panel is up **now**, before typing into it
- an application's window exists, instead of sleeping long enough that it
  usually does
- the active workspace or monitor is the one whose coordinates are about to be
  clicked
- the keyboard layout is the one the caller is about to type through — which is
  a state that may have been wrong all along and never produces a transition

Observable difference: a step whose prerequisite is not met stops the sequence
instead of letting later steps run against a screen the caller has misread. The
check becomes cheap enough that callers stop skipping it.

Four outcomes must be distinguishable, because collapsing them is how this
feature would recreate the problem it exists to solve:

| Result | Means |
|---|---|
| confirmed | the prerequisite held |
| not confirmed | it did not hold within the deadline — **not** that the action was undone |
| unavailable | ai-mirror could not observe; the caller learns nothing either way |
| cancelled | control was revoked, or the caller gave up |

## What this does not fix

- **It does not prove causation.** Another agent, the human, or the application
  itself can satisfy a prerequisite. Observing that a panel is open does not
  establish that this caller opened it.
- **It does not see inside a surface.** Which tab has focus, what a text field
  contains, which row the cursor is on — none of that is visible here, and the
  drifting tab count is what aimed the `Return` in the `azure-cli` failure.
  That belongs to olafkfreund/nixarchy#749, where letting `summon` take
  `{"query": …, "tab": …}` removes the sequence rather than reporting on it.
- **It does not make sequences atomic.** A confirmed state can change before
  the next action. This narrows a window; it does not close one.
- **It does not help a caller who does not use it,** or who ignores a negative
  answer. Opt-in checks are advice, not enforcement.
- **It is not primarily a speed win.** It can beat a conservative sleep, and it
  can also cost the full time a prerequisite takes.

## Affected users and systems

- **Any agent driving this desktop**, on any nixarchy host. The benefit is
  largest for scripted sequences — the ones fast enough to outrun the
  compositor.
- **ai-mirror gains a dependency on Hyprland's observable surfaces**, whichever
  are used: `hyprctl layers` / `clients` / `activeworkspace`, and possibly the
  event socket. These are a compositor-version contract that nothing here pins.
  0.56 is what runs on p620.
- **The ownership gate.** Observing is not acting, but a confirmation attached
  to a mutating sequence is not obviously the same thing as `index`. Open
  question below.
- **The user**, who currently pays for every silent mis-step — in the case that
  prompted this, with an unrequested edit to their NixOS configuration.

## Constraints

- **Must not break existing callers.** Confirmation is opt-in; everything that
  works today keeps working unchanged.
- **Must be bounded end to end**, including connecting and processing, and must
  stay responsive to cancellation. An agent blocked forever is worse than one
  that guessed, because it cannot be told it was wrong.
- **Must not present "not confirmed" as "did not happen".** The action may have
  landed late. Anything that retries on a timeout can double an effect, or
  toggle a panel shut that had just opened.
- **Must not present "unavailable" as "false".** A missing socket or a
  compositor restart makes confirmation unavailable, not negative.
- **Must not invent certainty about ordering.** Events can duplicate, arrive
  for causes the caller did not create, or be missed; a state that already
  holds produces no transition at all.
- **Must not leak connections.** This host already carries 15 `ai_mirror mcp`
  processes that outlived their sessions; it does not need leaked sockets too.
- **Must degrade**, never become fatal.
- **No new runtime dependencies**, stdlib Python, per house style.

## Open questions

1. **State query, event wait, or both?** `hyprctl layers` answers "is it up
   now" with no race and no subscription, and suits any prerequisite that
   persists. The event socket suits transitions that do not persist, and can
   wake a check rather than polling it. The first draft of this intent assumed
   events; that assumption is exactly what the review took apart. My reading is
   that state-checking is the primitive and events are an optimisation, but
   this is the central decision and belongs in the spec.

2. **Where does the check attach?** After the action (simple, composable, but
   loses anything that resolves before the caller asks); as a field on `input`
   (closes the ordering question, couples two concerns, grows the largest
   schema here); or arm-then-act-then-await with a token (closes it without the
   coupling, and adds expiry, cleanup and lifetime). Only the third survives a
   prerequisite that is transient, and it is not obviously worth that.

3. **Does confirmation require the control grant?** Observation is like
   `index`, which needs none. But a confirmation between two mutating steps is
   part of a mutating sequence, and revocation mid-wait must stop the sequence
   rather than let it resume.

4. **Does a failed confirmation stop a batch?** For an explicitly guarded
   sequence, stopping is the outcome being asked for, not a preference. Earlier
   actions stay applied; that needs saying plainly in the result.

5. **How much of the observable surface is exposed?** A caller nominating named
   prerequisites is narrow and safe. Handing back raw events or full client
   lists is more useful for diagnosis and names every window title on the
   machine — the privacy consideration #3 already ran into.

6. **Is any of this worth it versus fixing the callers?** A caller that checked
   `hyprctl layers` itself, today, would get most of this. The argument for
   building it here is that the check must be cheap and obvious or it will be
   skipped — which is an argument about ergonomics, not capability, and should
   be defended as one.
